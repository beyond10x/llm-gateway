# Owned-resource hosting verification — 2026-09-21

The [hosting contract](../hosting.md) defines the implemented ownership lifecycle and its limits.
Nothing in this checkpoint opens a socket, reads a credential or allocates a cloud resource;
`b10x-llm-provision` has no dependencies at all and its only provider is the in-process
`FakeProvider`.

## What the second correction round changed

Two adversarial passes ran against this domain. The first found five of six mutations surviving
the whole suite and was answered by measuring every published bound at its literal number and one
past it. The second found the first pass's blocker still reachable through a second slot, and six
further guards that survived deletion with everything green — *not* because a fixture could not
reach them, but because **no mutation had ever been aimed at them**. That distinction is the
lesson of this domain and is why the falsification record below is organised by the guard each
defect is aimed at rather than by the behaviour it breaks.

| Finding | Closed by |
| --- | --- |
| `Stop`'s foreign-resource refusal hidden behind an already-open reason | one precedence rule on the reason slot, stated in `record_stop_reason`, which is now the only writer of it, and asserted over every ordered pair of published reasons |
| the precedence rule asserted in neither direction | both directions measured — an ordinary reason does not displace one already recorded, `ownership-lost` always does — and both have a mutation aimed at them |
| `provision`'s phase guard, `fence`'s liveness check, `restore`'s requested-scope check, `adopt`'s scope guard, `settle_create`'s owner disjunct | one named case each, each killed by a mutation that deletes exactly that guard |
| `INTENTS` omits `Declared -> StopRequired` | the enumeration made machine-checkable: a grid derived from `Phase::ALL`, with a test requiring a row per cell, plus `require_stop`/`discharge` returning their refusal so a command cannot report `Ok` for a write the table refused |
| three guards that cannot be tripped | two deleted with the invariant that made them untrippable now asserted where it lives, one made the guard its command actually trips |
| the closing bound on `restore` stated more narrowly than the code behaves | corrected in the contract and below |
| a vacuously-true assertion in a rewritten case | that assertion and the two others of its shape enumerated and each made non-vacuous or removed |

## Counted coverage

The hosting lane has **65 scenarios**: 58 authored and seven generated, with zero synthesis
refusals. Three consecutive runs against restored sources each report
`{"total":65,"passed":65,"failed":0,"error":0,"unsupported":0,"skipped":0}`. The baseline holds
answered/total floors at 1 and a skipped ceiling of zero. No case was quarantined, ignored or
removed; two of the 58 authored scenarios are the adversary's, one from each pass, and both are
retained unchanged. The lane is selected with `--scenarios contracts/hosting/scenarios`; the
combined suite and `contracts/ess-inputs.yaml` are regenerated by the integration owner, and the
entries the combined suite needs are in the patch this unit handed over — **58 lines, the last of
them the second adversarial pass's scenario.**

```
ess verify conform synthesize --path spec --suite-format 5 --target ir \
  --scenarios contracts/hosting/scenarios --out target/hosting-suite.json
cargo run --locked -p b10x-llm-conformance -- \
  target/hosting-suite.json target/hosting-baseline.json target/hosting-run local
```

The [report](hosting-report.json) is the `report/2` artefact of the third of those runs. The
hosting target builds a real `Controller` over a real `LeaseRegistry` and a real `FakeProvider`,
invokes the public API, and reports the phases, refusal codes, provider call counts, leases,
observed identities and stop obligations those calls returned. It reads no scenario name and no
expected assertion to decide an answer, and it reimplements no lifecycle rule: every phase in
the facts came out of `Controller::view`, and the published transition table came out of
`llm_provision::TRANSITIONS`. Its notification and execution-record entities belong to the
verification adapter, not a production event bus.

The authored programs exercise the acceptance directly: provisioning (`Declare`/`Acquire`/
`Provision` against the real create path), readiness (a provider that reports ready, one that
reports not-ready and one that reports nothing), concurrent acquisition (eight real threads on a
`std::sync::Barrier` racing one lease), active leases (renewal, expiry, release and epoch
fencing), restart reconciliation (`snapshot`/`restore`, into a tightened policy as well as the
same one) and cleanup (requested stops, ambiguous stops, provider-confirmed stops,
inventory-absent settlement and external confirmation).

Fixtures are deliberately incomplete as well as complete. A provider that reports no readiness,
no address, no served model and no idempotency key is a first-class case, and so is a listing
that does not claim to be complete and a program that cannot be run at all.
`an-unrunnable-program-reports-absent-counts-not-zero-counts` asserts every provider counter is
`null`, and `an-invalid-policy-reports-zero-calls-because-the-provider-exists` asserts they are
`0` — the same four fields, one where the count does not exist and one where it does and is
nought.

## Every published bound, at the number and one past it

Five deliberate defects in the ceilings survived the first pass. None of them was subtle: the
resource ceiling multiplied by ten, its guard deleted outright, the two lifetime comparisons
loosened by one, and the effective-lifetime calculation inverted from minimum to maximum. They
survived because every fixture used the same ceiling value and the same lifetime pair, so no
fixture was ever *at* a boundary — only comfortably inside one.

The rule this now follows is: **every published bound is measured at the literal number and one
past it, and the expected value is written as a literal rather than derived from the constant
being tested.** A bound asserted as `CONSTANT + 1` cannot notice the constant moving. Each bound
in the contract and in the observation adapter, with where its pair lives:

| Bound | At the number | One past it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `MAX_ACTIVE_CEILING` (4096) | admitted | 4097 refused |
| `max_active` lower limit | 1 admitted | 0 refused |
| `max_active` occupied slots | the last slot admitted, `at_capacity` false | the next refused, `at_capacity` true |
| `max_lifetime_ms` lower limit | 1 admitted | 0 refused |
| `lease_ms` lower limit | 1 admitted | 0 refused |
| requested lifetime vs ceiling | exactly the ceiling admitted | one past refused |
| requested lifetime lower limit | 1 admitted | 0 refused |
| time-ceiling instant | no obligation at the deadline | obligation one millisecond later |
| `effective_lifetime_ms` | the tighter of the two, across a restart | — |
| lease expiry (inclusive) | live at `expires_at_ms` | expired one millisecond later |
| identifier length (256) | 256 admitted | 257 refused |
| adapter `MAX_STEPS` (128) | 128 steps run | 129 refused |
| adapter `MAX_CONCURRENT` (32) | 32 owners race | 33 refused |
| adapter `MAX_PROGRAM_BYTES` (65536) | 65536 admitted | 65537 refused |

The last three are checked by the conformance crate's own unit tests, which is why that lane no
longer reports zero cases.

## Rust checks

`cargo test -p b10x-llm-provision --locked --no-fail-fast` runs **93 cases**, in four binaries:
24 unit tests in `src/`, 57 behaviour tests in `tests/hosting.rs`, eight in
`tests/adversary_ownership.rs` and four in `tests/adversary_pass2.rs`. Every one of the four
prints its own `test result: ok` line and the totals above are read off those lines.
`cargo test -p b10x-llm-conformance --locked` runs seven adapter tests. `cargo clippy` on both
packages and `cargo fmt --check` on both are clean, and `ess specify validate --path spec`
reports `llm v1 — 10 file(s), valid`.

`every_unmapped_transition_is_refused` checks all 64 ordered phase pairs against an
independently written permitted set. Alongside it, `machine.rs` carries two tables of what the
controller actually asks for, because a phase write guarded on `may_become` and nothing else is
*silent* when the table forbids it — which is how a foreign owner tag came to be ignored, and how
`RequestStop` on a `Declared` record came to answer `Ok` while recording nothing.

* `INTENTS` is the narrative list, one row per change a **conditional** writer asks for, each
  marked permitted or a deliberate no-op. A row that flips without being edited fails.
* `OBLIGATION_INTENTS` is a grid: `Phase::ALL` against the three targets the writers that take no
  phase precondition ask for — `require_stop`, `discharge` and `cancel`.
  `the_three_unconditional_writers_are_enumerated_from_every_phase` derives that grid from the
  vocabulary and requires exactly one row per cell. This is the check the narrative table never
  had, and its absence is why `Declared -> StopRequired` could be missing from a table whose
  whole claim is completeness. A phase added to `Phase` fails here until its three rows exist.

`every_obligation_is_expressible_from_every_phase_that_can_hold_a_resource` is the standing form
of the same rule. Underneath both, `require_stop` and `discharge` now return whether the write
happened, and `RequestStop` and `ConfirmStopped` refuse with `wrong-phase` when it did not, so
the silent-`Ok` symptom is not expressible for a command at all.

`HostingError`, `Phase`, `StopReason`, `Dispatch`, `ProviderState`, `Idempotency`,
`Completeness` and `AmbiguousCreate` are generated by one macro from a single variant list, so
each type's `ALL` inventory and `code` mapping cannot fall behind its variants inside Rust. Two
checks carry that past the crate boundary rather than leaving the gap implicit.
`every_vocabulary_is_declared_identically_in_rust_and_in_the_specification` reads
`spec/domains/hosting.yaml` and compares its four re-declared variant lists against the Rust
inventories. `every_published_refusal_is_emitted_by_some_real_sequence_of_calls` and its
counterpart for `StopReason` produce every variant through real public calls under an exhaustive
match, so a value published without a way to reach it does not compile. What is still not
checked, and is stated in [the contract](../hosting.md) rather than left to be discovered: the
view's `DeploymentFact.phase` and `.stop_reason` are typed `String` in the specification, as
every other domain in this repository types its view facts, so ESS does not validate an observed
value against the declared variants.

standing red on a precondition it believed unsatisfiable. That case was rewritten by the

## Qualification boundary

These fixtures prove the implemented ownership policy over a provider that behaves exactly as
the fixture says. They do not prove that any real control plane behaves that way, that a
provider's listing is truthful or complete when it claims to be, that a provider's termination
report means billing stopped, or that any cloud account was contacted at all. No paid call runs,
and the crate cannot make one.

One more thing is read and not claimed: `Controller::restore` refuses an instant below what its
own snapshot witnessed, and the bound is narrower than an earlier wording here made it sound. A
record witnesses an instant only if it carries a submission instant or an observation, so a
`Declared` or `Cancelled` record raises the floor by nothing — which means a *non-empty* snapshot
made only of such records constrains the clock exactly as little as an empty one does. No harm
could be constructed from it: such a record has no started instant to un-fire and `restore` drops
the lease regardless. The contract now states it that way, and no test pretends otherwise.

Runpod and Modal adapters, their per-capability control-plane evidence, and live paid
qualification remain separate stories. The gateway must still route hosting effects through the
`llm-cost` ledger and consume the stop obligations this contract reports; that integration is
also outstanding. The combined-suite and baseline regeneration, and the entries in
`contracts/ess-inputs.yaml`, belong to the integration owner and were not made by this unit.
